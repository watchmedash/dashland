Where points come from

Firsts

Points are counted from what you have done, so nothing is ever lost — and what you spend them on is yours for good. It does not wear out, and you do not drop it when you die.

Settings

A full day and night takes as long as day length says. Set it to zero and the planet follows your device clock instead — pretty, but a cycle then lasts a real day, so you would rarely meet what comes out after dark. Weather changes on its own. There is a single rendering configuration — drop render scale below 1.0 if you need more performance.

Your left hand is the slot beside the hotbar. Drag anything into it in the inventory, or press F to trade it for whatever you are holding. It carries and it shows — a torch in it burns and lights your way — but everything is used from the right hand, so F is how you use what is in it.
Dig soil with a shovel to make farmland, sow seeds on it, and harvest the wheat once it ripens — farmland near water grows twice as fast.
Items on the ground are picked up automatically when you walk near them. A fishing rod is three sticks and two hides. Aim at open water and click to cast, then watch the float: when it dips, click again. Strike too early or too late and it is gone. Fish grill on a kiln.
A door is six planks and stands two blocks tall — right-click to swing it. Shut, it seals a doorway against the night; open, you walk straight through.
Ladders are seven sticks for three. Hang them on a wall and hold Space to climb, Ctrl to go down; let go and you simply hang there. Catching one cancels a fall.
The year turns every three days — spring, summer, autumn, winter, shown next to the weather. Crops race through spring and barely stir in winter, so sow early; in deep winter the rain falls as snow wherever you are, not just in the north.
Fences are four planks and two sticks for three, and they join themselves up as you place them. One stands higher than a block, so nothing walks or hops over a line of them — a ring of fence keeps a herd where you left it, and you can still see across it.
A sign is six planks and a stick, for three. Place one and type a line; after that just look at it to read it, so a row of signs at a junction reads itself as you turn.
Build a bed early and use it: you wake up there when you die, and on a planet this size that is the difference between losing a night and losing your house. It does not skip the night — the planet keeps your own clock, so its evening is your evening.
The planet has a core, about thirty layers down through basalt and lava. Reach it and it gives you a hearth — the only one there will ever be. Set it down and nothing will spawn for a long way around it, which is what turns a camp into a home.
The planet keeps your clock, so a new world can begin at any hour. If yours begins after dark you get a few torches and three quiet minutes to find your feet — after that the night is yours to deal with.
Husks come out after dark, and out of any unlit space at any hour. Torches keep them out — light a mine as you dig it and nothing will spawn behind you.
You get tougher by playing, not by carrying. Press K for Growth: six branches — health, damage tolerance, agility, lungs, hands and reach — bought with points earned from everything you have already done, plus a few for firsts like reaching the core or putting down a husk. Nothing in it wears out, nothing is dropped when you die, and you can unlearn the lot and spend the points again for free. Three branches sit behind another, so the first dozen points are a choice about who you are.
A crate holds twenty-seven stacks — shift-click to move a whole stack in or out, and break one to get everything back. Crates you find already standing in the world have something in them. Listen for a bell: it means a wandering merchant is somewhere within earshot. He buys almost anything and sells a pack of oddments — click a line to trade one, shift-click for ten. Most traders are also after something, and pay well over the odds for it — bring what he asks and hand it over at the top of his screen. Each trader wants his own thing, so the next one is a new errand. He carries what he carries, and he only has so many coins, so sell the good stuff while he can still pay. In the inventory, the Can Craft sidebar lists everything your materials allow — click to craft one, shift-click to craft as many as you can.
